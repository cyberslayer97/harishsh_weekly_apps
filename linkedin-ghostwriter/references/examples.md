## Example 1: Lesson learned

I automated my weekly report and made it worse.

The goal was simple: stop copying numbers into a spreadsheet every Friday.

So I connected five tools, added a schedule, and called it done.

Two weeks later nobody had opened the report. It was accurate, but it arrived at 3 a.m. with no summary.

I removed three of the five steps and added one line at the top: what changed since last week.

Open rate went from nobody to everyone.

Automation saves time only if the output still gets used.

## Example 2: Contrarian take

"Learn every AI tool" is bad advice.

Tools change every month. The skill that lasts is breaking a task into steps a machine can follow.

Most people fail at the breakdown, not the tool.

Pick one workflow you repeat weekly. Write each step in plain words. Then decide which steps need judgment and which don't.

The tool choice takes ten minutes after that.

The exception: if your job is to evaluate tools, then yes, learn them all.
